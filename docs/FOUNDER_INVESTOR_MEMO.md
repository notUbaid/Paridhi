# Paridhi: founder and investor memo

A managed protection service for vacant land in India owned by people living abroad. This memo sets out the thesis,
the offer and the unit economics, and marks which numbers are assumptions still to be tested.

## 1. The problem

Many non-resident Indians own vacant plots in fast-growing corridors around Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Mohali, the NCR,
Pune and Ahmedabad. From abroad they cannot easily see the land, visit the revenue office or respond quickly to a
dispute. The common failure modes:

- **Physical encroachment** that starts small: dumped material, a moved boundary peg, a temporary shed.
- **Paper fraud**: a mutation application at the revenue office based on a forged deed or false inheritance claim.
- **Slow remedies**: once possession or the record has changed, civil litigation can take many years.

**Market size (to be sourced before sharing externally).** Earlier drafts quoted "$1.2 trillion of NRI real estate",
"68% vacant plots" and "3.2 million addressable parcels". None of these has a cited source. Replace them with figures
from the Ministry of External Affairs, RBI remittance data, state registration statistics or a commissioned survey.

## 2. Why existing options fall short

- **Property managers and relatives** visit irregularly, with no structured record and no legal follow-through.
- **Consumer cameras** need mains power and Wi-Fi, which vacant plots lack.
- **Lawyers alone** act only after the owner finds out, which is often late.

Paridhi's bet is that the combination matters: a camera for continuous coverage, a person on the ground for what a
camera can't judge, a regular check of the land record, and an advocate ready to act, all in one record the owner can
see and share.

## 3. The offer

**₹4,999 a month per plot (proposed; GST treatment to be confirmed).** Includes:

- Solar 4G camera kit installed on a 4.5 m pole, with data SIM.
- Scheduled snapshots, motion clips and a weekly change comparison.
- Monthly field inspection with geotagged photos; extra visits on request.
- Regular checks of the state land record for mutation applications.
- Retained local advocate: caveat in the civil court, renewed every 90 days, and objections to flagged mutations.

## 4. Unit economics (planning assumptions)

| Item | Per plot per year | Basis |
| :--- | ---: | :--- |
| Revenue | ₹59,988 | ₹4,999 × 12 |
| Camera kit, amortised | ₹5,950 | ₹17,850 kit (see HARDWARE_EDGE_SPEC.md) over 36 months |
| Data SIM and cloud storage | ₹2,160 | ₹180 / month |
| Field inspections | ₹7,200 | ₹600 × 12 visits |
| Advocate retainer | ₹4,800 | ₹400 / month |
| **Direct cost** | **₹20,110** | |
| **Gross margin** | **about 66%** | |

Not yet included, and likely to lower the margin:

- Court and filing fees for four caveat renewals a year, possibly in more than one court.
- Advocate time for objections when a mutation is flagged.
- Inspector travel to plots far from a city, and the minimum plot density needed per inspector.
- Replacement after theft or storm damage, and site maintenance.
- Customer support, payment processing, and GST.

**Retention is unknown.** Earlier drafts claimed under 1.5% annual churn; there is no data for that yet. Measure it in
the pilot.

## 5. What has to be true

1. Owners will pay a monthly fee for prevention, not only for a crisis.
2. Inspectors can reach enough plots per day in a corridor to make ₹600 a visit work.
3. Advocates will take a retainer at roughly ₹400 a month per plot and renew caveats reliably.
4. Land record checks catch flagged applications within the objection window often enough to matter.

## 6. Go-to-market

- **Start in one corridor** (for example Shamshabad and the Hyderabad Outer Ring Road) to build inspector density,
  with 10–20 pilot plots.
- **Channels:** cross-border chartered accountants and wealth advisers, diaspora associations, and conveyancing
  lawyers in the target cities.
- **Pilot metrics:** cost per visit, time to detect a test change on site, check-to-alert time for registry flags,
  caveat renewals on time, monthly retention.
